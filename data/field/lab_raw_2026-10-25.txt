2. Nitrite: is the figure reported as NO2- or as NO2-N? The two differ by a

ANSWERS, 2026-11-01

1. Hardness units: ANSWERED by a photograph of the test-strip bottle.

   The strip is a Total Hardness (as CaCO3) strip with a six-level colour
   chart carrying two columns:

       ppm:   0    25    50   120   250   425
       gpg:   0   1.5     3     7    15    25
              SOFT -------- HARD ------ VERY HARD

   The reported set was 25, 25, 25, 7, 15, 25. The values 7 and 15 do not
   appear in the ppm column at all; both appear in the gpg column. The gpg
   column was therefore the one being read, and "25" means 25 gpg, not
   25 ppm.

   Converted with the manufacturer's own chart:

       sample 1   25 gpg = 425 ppm CaCO3   VERY HARD
       sample 2   25 gpg = 425 ppm CaCO3   VERY HARD
       sample 3   25 gpg = 425 ppm CaCO3   VERY HARD
       sample 4    7 gpg = 120 ppm CaCO3   HARD
       sample 5   15 gpg = 250 ppm CaCO3   VERY HARD
       sample 6   25 gpg = 425 ppm CaCO3   VERY HARD

   NOTE: 425 ppm is the top level of the chart. Four of the six samples sit
   there, so those readings are right-censored: the true hardness is at least
   425 ppm and the strip cannot say how much more. Differences in hardness
   between samples 1, 2, 3 and 6 are not resolvable with this strip.

2. Nitrite species: ANSWERED. The instrument reported NO2-, not NO2-N.
   The figures are therefore directly comparable with the WHO provisional
   guideline value of 3 mg/L, which is stated for the ion.

3. Chlorine units: ANSWERED. mg/L.

4. Zeros: ANSWERED. A reported 0 means the sample read the same as distilled
   water, i.e. indistinguishable from the blank. These are non-detects, not
   true zeros, and are now stored with below_lod=true. The numeric detection
   limit itself was not supplied, so `lod` stays empty.

5. Sulfate methods: ANSWERED, with a caveat about the identification.

   Both figures come from the same chemistry: sulfate is precipitated with
   barium chloride as barium sulfate, and the instrument derives the
   concentration from the resulting turbidity. The two differ in how the
   reagent is delivered.

     - Square cuvettes with a powder reagent. If the instrument is a Hach,
       this is Method 8051 with SulfaVer 4, based on USEPA 375.4. Working
       range about 2-70 mg/L SO4. The blank is zeroed first, the reagent
       added, then about five minutes' development.
     - Barcoded tubes, i.e. cuvette tests, with the reagent pre-filled and
       the barcode selecting the calibration. For Hach these are LCK 153
       (40-150 mg/L) or LCK 353 (150-900 mg/L).

   CAVEAT: the instrument itself was not identified. If it was a Merck
   Spectroquant rather than a Hach, the equivalents are the Spectroquant
   Sulfate Test and the Spectroquant Sulfate Cell Test. The chemistry and
   the argument below are the same either way, but the exact method
   designations are provisional until the instrument is confirmed.

   CONSEQUENCE: of the six samples, only sample 4 (54 mg/L by the powder
   method) falls inside the cuvette test's 40-150 mg/L window. The other
   five, at 2-9 mg/L, are below its lower limit and cannot be measured by
   it at all. That is why exactly one sample was re-measured -- and it means
   the 17.8% gap observed at sample 4 cannot be transferred to the others as
   a correction. Checking them would need a different reference method, not
   this one.
