1. Hardness units. "25" is reported without a unit. 25 mg/L CaCO3 is very soft

   readings. Until this is answered the hardness column cannot be interpreted

2. Nitrite: is the figure reported as NO2- or as NO2-N? The two differ by a
   factor of 3.28, and the distinction decides whether the values sit above or
   below the WHO guideline.

3. Chlorine units -- presumed mg/L, not stated.

4. Methods and detection limits for every analyte. Several results are 0.00,
   which is almost certainly "below the detection limit" rather than a true
   zero; without the limit they cannot be recorded correctly (see

ANSWERS, 2026-11-01

1. Hardness units: ANSWERED by a photograph of the test-strip bottle.

   The strip is a Total Hardness (as CaCO3) strip with a six-level colour
   chart carrying two columns:

       ppm:   0    25    50   120   250   425
       gpg:   0   1.5     3     7    15    25
              SOFT -------- HARD ------ VERY HARD

   The reported set was 25, 25, 25, 7, 15, 25. The values 7 and 15 do not
   appear in the ppm column at all; both appear in the gpg column. The gpg
   column was therefore the one being read, and "25" means 25 gpg, not
   25 ppm.

   Converted with the manufacturer's own chart:

       sample 1   25 gpg = 425 ppm CaCO3   VERY HARD
       sample 2   25 gpg = 425 ppm CaCO3   VERY HARD
       sample 3   25 gpg = 425 ppm CaCO3   VERY HARD
       sample 4    7 gpg = 120 ppm CaCO3   HARD
       sample 5   15 gpg = 250 ppm CaCO3   VERY HARD
       sample 6   25 gpg = 425 ppm CaCO3   VERY HARD

   NOTE: 425 ppm is the top level of the chart. Four of the six samples sit
   there, so those readings are right-censored: the true hardness is at least
   425 ppm and the strip cannot say how much more. Differences in hardness
   between samples 1, 2, 3 and 6 are not resolvable with this strip.

2. Nitrite species: ANSWERED. The instrument reported NO2-, not NO2-N.
   The figures are therefore directly comparable with the WHO provisional
   guideline value of 3 mg/L, which is stated for the ion.

3. Chlorine units: ANSWERED. mg/L.

4. Zeros: ANSWERED. A reported 0 means the sample read the same as distilled
   water, i.e. indistinguishable from the blank. These are non-detects, not
   true zeros, and are now stored with below_lod=true. The numeric detection
   limit itself was not supplied, so `lod` stays empty.

5. Sulfate methods: ANSWERED, with a caveat about the identification.

   Both figures come from the same chemistry: sulfate is precipitated with
   barium chloride as barium sulfate, and the instrument derives the
   concentration from the resulting turbidity. The two differ in how the
   reagent is delivered.

     - Square cuvettes with a powder reagent. If the instrument is a Hach,
       this is Method 8051 with SulfaVer 4, based on USEPA 375.4. Working
       range about 2-70 mg/L SO4. The blank is zeroed first, the reagent
       added, then about five minutes' development.
     - Barcoded tubes, i.e. cuvette tests, with the reagent pre-filled and
       the barcode selecting the calibration. For Hach these are LCK 153
       (40-150 mg/L) or LCK 353 (150-900 mg/L).

   CAVEAT: the instrument itself was not identified. If it was a Merck
   Spectroquant rather than a Hach, the equivalents are the Spectroquant
   Sulfate Test and the Spectroquant Sulfate Cell Test. The chemistry and
   the argument below are the same either way, but the exact method
   designations are provisional until the instrument is confirmed.

   CONSEQUENCE: of the six samples, only sample 4 (54 mg/L by the powder
   method) falls inside the cuvette test's 40-150 mg/L window. The other
   five, at 2-9 mg/L, are below its lower limit and cannot be measured by
   it at all. That is why exactly one sample was re-measured -- and it means
   the 17.8% gap observed at sample 4 cannot be transferred to the others as
   a correction. Checking them would need a different reference method, not
   this one.

10. Programme numbers on the DR 3900, as supplied:

        nitrite   373
        sulfate   680
        phosphate LCK 549   (not an analyte of this project)
        iron      "нету" - no iron programme on the instrument

11. NITRITE WAS MEASURED ON THE WRONG RANGE. This is the most consequential
    finding of the whole batch and it invalidates a claim this repository
    was making.

    Hach programme 373 is nitrite HIGH RANGE: the NitriVer 2 ferrous-sulfate
    method, scaled to 250 mg/L NO2-, with a stated sensitivity of 1.4 mg/L
    NO2- per 0.010 absorbance and a 95% interval of 191-209 around a 200 mg/L
    standard.

    The samples read 2-7 mg/L. That is the bottom 3% of the method's span,
    where one sensitivity step is 1.4 mg/L -- roughly half the WHO guideline
    value the readings were being compared against. Consequences:

      - The reported integers 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 are about one to five
        sensitivity steps above zero. They are not resolved measurements.
      - The "first reading highest" bias of 1-2 mg/L is the same size as one
        sensitivity step, so that pattern may simply be the method's
        behaviour at its floor rather than a procedural fault.
      - Nothing here establishes whether any spring is above or below
        3 mg/L. The comparison with the WHO provisional guideline must be
        withdrawn, not merely caveated.

    What is needed: the LOW RANGE nitrite method, diazotization with
    NitriVer 3 (Hach programme 371), which works in the 0.002-0.300 mg/L
    NO2-N band appropriate to natural groundwater.

12. Sulfate programme 680 is the SulfaVer 4 powder-pillow method at
    2-70 mg/L with a 10 mL sample. All six samples (2-54 mg/L) fall inside
    that range, so sulfate -- unlike nitrite -- was measured on an
    appropriate scale. This also confirms the earlier deduction: only
    sample 4 was re-run on the barcoded cuvette test, the other five stayed
    on the spectrophotometer, exactly as the ranges predicted.

13. IRON HAS NO RECORDED METHOD. The laboratory states there is no iron
    programme on the DR 3900, yet six iron results were reported. The
    instrument and method behind the iron column are therefore unknown, and
    the column cannot be relied on until that is answered. It is marked
    accordingly rather than deleted: the readings exist and may be perfectly
    good, but their provenance does not.

14. pH was measured on a Metrohm 913 pH Meter with a Pt1000 temperature
    sensor, confirmed by photograph -- not on the DR 3900. Calibration date
    still not supplied.

15. Hardness by test strip, confirmed by photograph: the six strips are
    visible beside the six sample tubes. Recorded separately from the
    instrumental methods.

STILL MISSING:

  - How iron was actually measured (see 13).
  - pH electrode calibration date.
  - Reagent lot numbers and expiry dates.
  - A low-range nitrite measurement, which is now a re-analysis request
    rather than a documentation request.
