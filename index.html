Six springs above Almaty

On 25 October 2026 six springs on the northern slope of the Ile-Alatau were located from 34 geotagged photographs and sampled. A university teaching laboratory returned 151 readings across nine analytes. Six of the nine can be used, three cannot, and one spring turned out unlike the other five.

One spring is unlike the other five

Spring 4 (ALA-0004) has the highest pH (8.15), the most sulfate (54 mg/L against 2 to 9 elsewhere), the softest water (120 mg/L where four others read at least 425) and the warmest water: 12.5 °C on an afternoon when the air was −1 °C and the other springs ran at 1.5 to 8 °C. A municipal sign at the outlet reads “Hydrogen sulfide spring, 1435 m”; the elevation model puts it at 1436 m.

Sulfate rising while hardness falls is not what dissolving gypsum does. A sulfide spring offers another source for it, since sulfate is what sulfide oxidises to, and together with the warmth and the pH that is the pattern of water that has travelled a deeper path. It remains a hypothesis one visit cannot test: sulfide, sodium, calcium and conductivity were not measured.

Bars and dots are the mean of three readings and the ticks are the readings themselves. Chlorine is not charted: 17 of its 18 readings were indistinguishable from distilled water.

This is not a safety assessment. No microbiological testing was done, and what makes spring water dangerous is usually microbiological. The comparison for iron and nitrite is withdrawn, because those measurements cannot support one.

Six of nine analytes were measured by a method that fits spring water

pH, sulfate, copper and turbidity came through as reported, chlorine as a clean non-detect, and hardness with its top readings cut off by the strip’s scale. Nitrite, ammonium and iron did not: the tests the laboratory holds are built for water far stronger than a mountain spring, and nothing in the numbers themselves gave that away.

pH was read on a Metrohm 913 meter and turbidity on a VELP TB1 turbidimeter; neither is a range-limited colour test, so they are not in this comparison. Sulfate at spring 4 was also run on a second method. It read 65.7 mg/L against 54, a gap of 18%, which is the only measure of bias this batch contains.

The first reading was the highest in 23 of 33 triplicates

That is among the 33 triplicates whose three readings were not all identical. If the order of readings did not matter, the first would be the highest in about a third of them, 11; the chance of 23 or more is 0.00002. Turbidity shows one cause, particles settling in the cuvette between readings. A finding like this exists only because the laboratory ran its measurements in triplicate.
